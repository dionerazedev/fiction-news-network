Every morning for a month, the staff at the Cobb Street Branch Library in Millbrook, Ohio found the same thing in the book return bin: a stack of overnight returns, a few overdue DVDs and, sitting on top of them, a large orange cat.

"He'd just look at us," said branch manager Priya Halvorsen. "Like, *you're late.*"

Nobody knew how he was getting in. The book return is a metal slot in the outside wall with a chute that drops books into a padded bin inside. The opening is barely wider than a hardcover. It turns out that's wide enough.

A security camera finally caught him at 11:52 one night: a flash of orange fur diving headfirst into the slot, followed by a muffled thump and, about thirty seconds later, the sound of contented purring.

## Why the chute?

The library's best guess is warmth. The book bin sits next to a heating vent, and the cushioned bottom is softer than anything a stray could find outside in November.

"He wasn't trying to break in," Priya said. "He was trying to get to bed."

The staff named him Dewey, after the decimal system. They took him to a vet, who found no microchip, gave him his shots and pronounced him about six years old and "extremely confident." He was adopted by the branch, with permission from the city, and moved into a cat bed behind the circulation desk.

He still prefers the bin.

## The Overdue Officer

Here is where Dewey's story gets unusual.

The Cobb Street Branch, like many libraries, had a problem with unreturned books. More than a thousand were years overdue. Letters and emails went unanswered.

In January a children's librarian, Mateo Fairweather, made a poster as a joke. It showed Dewey sitting in the return bin above the words *Inspector Dewey Is Waiting For Your Overdue Books.* He put it on the library's social media page.

The post was shared across the neighborhood within a day. People started returning books just to meet him.

> "A man came in with a book that was due in 2011. He said, 'I'm not here for the library, I'm here for the cat.' I said, 'That's fine, the cat accepts.'" — Mateo Fairweather

## By the numbers

In the nine months since the poster went up, more than 400 long-overdue books have come back. The oldest was a cookbook checked out in 1994. One family returned eleven books in a single wagon, each with a hand-drawn thank-you note to the cat.

The library has since cleared all late fees for anything returned while "Inspector Dewey is on duty," which, the staff points out, is always.

Dewey now has a small laminated badge on his collar that reads *Overdue Officer, Cobb Street Branch.* He sits on the circulation desk most afternoons, accepting returns with the dignity of a judge.

The chute has been fitted with a soft flap so no more cats can climb in. Dewey is reportedly unbothered. He is the only cat on staff, and he likes it that way.
